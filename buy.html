For buyers

See the house clearly, and decide without pressure.

Buying a home is a handful of big decisions, most of them on a deadline. I help you see a house for what it really is, act at the right moments, and get to closing without the expensive surprises — with your interests the only ones I'm looking after.

How I work with buyers

When you're buying, the listing agent works for the seller. What you want is someone on the other side of that table, whose only job is you: reading the house, running the numbers, and negotiating on your behalf.

That part I do with you directly, from the first showing to the closing table. Once you're under contract, a transaction coordinator keeps the paperwork and the dates moving so nothing slips, and I stay on the parts that call for judgment.

Where to start

Start with what you actually want

Before we look at a single listing, it's worth getting clear on how you want to live, what you'll trade off, and what you won't. The market has a way of talking people into a house that photographs well and lives badly.

Some of the most useful early conversations end with “not this one, keep looking.” That's a good result. The house you buy because the search felt long is the one you end up regretting.

Seeing it clearly

What the listing doesn't tell you

A listing is a sales document. Part of my job is to read past it — the condition the photos flatter, the work a house will likely need, and the questions worth asking before you fall for it.

I can give you a practical read on what a house looks like it needs and roughly what that tends to cost, so the inspection confirms things instead of blindsiding you. That's process knowledge, not an inspection. For anything structural or technical you want a licensed inspector or engineer, and I'll tell you which one to call.

The deadlines that matter

A contract runs on deadlines, and most of them protect you

Once your offer is accepted, the contract sets a series of deadlines — dated stretches of time to inspect, verify, renegotiate, or step away without losing your deposit. Each one is leverage while it's open. Each one is also enforceable: once the date passes, that right is generally gone, and a missed deadline can cost you the deposit or the position you were counting on.

Keeping track of every one of those dates is its own job, and a real part of the value of having representation. Between me and the transaction coordinator on your file, none of them slips — so a missed deadline is never the thing that costs you.

Negotiation

A deal you'd make again

Price is one part of it. The rest is the terms — what gets fixed before closing, what credits change hands, how much room the dates give you. A good outcome is one you'd agree to again knowing what you know now.

I'm a certified Real Estate Negotiation Expert, and a fair amount of the work is keeping a deal calm and moving when it would otherwise stall over something small.

To closing

Fewer surprises between contract and keys

Most of what goes wrong between contract and closing is a date missed, a document late, or a question raised too close to the end. The work here is unglamorous, and it's a good deal of the value: staying ahead of the lender, the title company, and the calendar so closing day is quiet.

If something does come up, you'll hear it from me early, with what it means and the options in front of you, not the afternoon before closing.
