The out-of-state checklist

Four questions worth answering early.

Most take a phone call to settle. Left alone, any one of them can get expensive.

Insurance
Many policies limit coverage once a home sits vacant 30 to 60 days. Call the insurer and ask.
Security
Mail piling up and an overgrown yard show a house is empty. A regular check keeps small problems small.
Storm season
June through November, someone local should be able to secure shutters, clear gutters, and check for damage afterward.
Bills
Keep power and water on so the house stays dry and cool, and keep taxes and HOA dues from lapsing.
If you decide to sell

It can all be done from home

  1. See it from home

    A video walk-through and dated photos, so you know the condition without a flight.

  2. Local eyes on it

    Regular checks, and vendors met on site: lawn, locksmith, cleanout, repairs.

  3. Sign from anywhere

    Listing papers and most documents are signed electronically.

  4. Close by mail

    Title companies routinely close with remote or mobile signing.

How I can help

Practical help now, options when you’re ready.

  • Property checks: dated photos of the outside, and the inside when you can arrange access.
  • Local vendors: locksmith, lawn care, cleanout, estate sale, handyman. I can meet them on site.
  • Value and options: what the home is worth, and what keeping, renting, or selling it looks like, as-is or after some work.
