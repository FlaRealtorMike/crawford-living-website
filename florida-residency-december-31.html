Moving to Florida

Moving to Florida? Why December 31, 2026 matters

Under Amendment 3, the date you become a Florida resident decides which homestead exemption you start with. It turns on where you live on December 31, not on when you buy a house.

The short version

Amendment 3 creates two groups of Florida homeowners. Which group you are in depends on one fact: were you a permanent Florida resident on December 31, 2026?

Resident by Dec 31, 2026
Up to $150,000 off non-school property taxes in 2027, and up to $250,000 from 2028, once you own a home here, live in it, and file for homestead
Resident from Jan 1, 2027
Start at the existing $50,000 (adjusted for inflation from 2028) for four years. The larger exemption applies from January 1 of your fifth year of homestead
School taxes
The $25,000 exemption is the same for both groups

Do I have to buy a house by December 31?

No. The test is when Florida became your permanent home, not when you closed. County property appraisers, including Sarasota’s and Pinellas’s, describe it the same way: a person who is a Florida resident by December 31, 2026 can get the larger exemption whenever they later buy and file for homestead.

So a family that moves here in December and rents for a year is in the first group. When they buy in 2027 and file for January 1, 2028, the $250,000 non-school exemption applies.

Closing before year-end only adds one thing: the 2027 tax year itself, and only if you are living in the home on January 1, 2027.

What does “resident” mean here?

It means you actually live here and intend to stay. Buying a house does not make you a resident, and neither does signing a lease on its own. Property appraisers look at the whole picture, and these are the documents they usually ask about:

  1. A Florida driver license, and your vehicles registered here.
  2. Florida voter registration.
  3. A Florida address on your federal tax return and your bank and mail.
  4. A Declaration of Domicile filed with the county clerk, which many new residents use.

Paperwork that doesn’t match where you live works against you. Closing in December and then spending the winter up north does not make you a resident on December 31. If an appraiser later finds an exemption wasn’t earned, they can look back up to ten years.

How much is the difference?

Take a home with a $400,000 assessed value in 2028:

Resident by Dec 31, 2026
$400,000 − $250,000 = $150,000 taxed by the county, city and other non-school districts
Resident from 2027
$400,000 − about $50,000 = about $350,000 taxed by those same districts

That is roughly $200,000 more taxable value. Each mill is $1 per $1,000, so every mill of non-school tax on that gap is about $200 a year. If the non-school rates where you buy added up to 12 mills, that’s about $2,400 a year, for four years. Rates differ by city and change every year; your county property appraiser’s estimator will show yours.

If your move is in 2027 anyway

That can still be the right call. A job that starts in March, a school year, a house up north that has to sell first: those are real reasons, and four years at the smaller exemption is a cost, not a penalty for life. It is one number in the budget, not the whole decision.

If you can move before year-end and it fits your life, it is worth knowing the date exists. If it doesn’t fit, plan your monthly cost on the $50,000 figure for the first four years and you won’t be surprised.

The three dates

December 31, 2026
Decides which group you are in
January 1 (each year)
You must own the home and live in it as your permanent residence on this date to get homestead for that year
March 1 (each year)
File with your county property appraiser. Filing is free. Having until March 1 to file doesn’t let you close in February and claim that year

We work in Orange, Seminole, Osceola, Lake and Volusia counties, and each has its own property appraiser’s office. For the full picture of what Amendment 3 changes, see Amendment 3 passed: Florida’s new homestead exemption.

What’s still open

The Legislature is expected to set the implementing details, including how earlier residency is documented, in its 2027 session. Some attorneys also expect the newcomer rule to be challenged in court, since the U.S. Supreme Court has struck down residency-date tax benefits before. Plan on the rule as written, and check again before you file.

Read this carefully

General information only, not legal or tax advice. Your county property appraiser decides homestead eligibility. For your own residency questions, talk with a Florida attorney or tax professional.
