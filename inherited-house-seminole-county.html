Where it's filed

Where probate is filed in Seminole County

Probate is filed in the county where the person lived. For Seminole County, that is the Seminole County Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller, in the Eighteenth Judicial Circuit. The clerk's probate office is at Sanford. The clerk's main number is 407-665-4300. The clerk lists probate services at particular Sanford buildings, and those can change, so a call before a trip saves a wasted drive.

The clerk's court records search shows the case once it is filed. Seminole shares its judicial circuit with Brevard County, so a Seminole case is heard in the Eighteenth Circuit, not the Ninth.

An attorney files the case for most families. If there is no attorney yet, the first call is to a Florida probate attorney, not to a real estate agent. Once there is one, I work to the attorney's timeline.

Who owns it

How to look up the owner, the homestead and the deed

The Seminole County Property Appraiser website shows the owner of record, whether the home has a homestead exemption, and the last recorded transfer. The clerk's official records show the recorded deed, any mortgage and any lien. Together they answer the first question a family has: whose name is on the home, and does it need probate to move?

A home held in a trust, under a lady bird deed, or jointly with survivorship often passes without a probate case. A home titled only in the name of the person who passed away generally does not. The Central Florida inherited-house guide walks through each case.

Selling an inherited or probate house in Seminole County: what tends to matter

Seminole County has a mix of older neighborhoods and newer planned communities. Older homes tend to raise questions about roof age, wiring and plumbing before listing, and newer communities tend to raise questions about association fees and rules. Both are worth knowing before an estate chooses between selling as-is and preparing the home. In Florida, a house can often be sold while the probate case is still open, once the personal representative has the authority to sign; the estate's attorney confirms when that is.

Step by step

Selling a house still in probate in Seminole County, step by step

Florida does not make a family wait for the probate case to close before selling a house. What the sale needs is someone with legal authority to sign the deed. Here is the usual order in Seminole County. Your attorney confirms each step for your estate.

  1. Check how the house was titled. Look up the parcel with the Seminole County Property Appraiser and the deed in the clerk’s official records. A house in the owner’s name alone generally goes through probate; a house in a trust, under a lady bird deed or owned jointly with survivorship often does not.
  2. The case is opened with the Seminole County Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller in Sanford, in the Eighteenth Judicial Circuit. Summary administration, for smaller estates, often takes a few weeks to a few months. Formal administration is filed by a Florida attorney and more often runs six to twelve months in total.
  3. A personal representative is appointed. In formal administration, the court appoints a personal representative and the clerk issues Letters of Administration. Until then, no one can sign a deed for the estate, and a title company won’t close without them.
  4. Check the will for a power of sale. If the will gives the personal representative power to sell real property, the sale usually needs no court order. If there is no will, or no usable power of sale, the court has to authorize or confirm the sale, so the contract should allow time for that order.
  5. If it was the owner’s homestead, the path changes. A protected homestead passes to the people Florida law names, and they generally sign the deed. The court can issue an order naming them and their shares. Tell the Seminole County Property Appraiser about the change in ownership, because the homestead exemption and its Save Our Homes cap don’t carry over to the new owners.
  6. The creditor period doesn’t have to hold up the sale. Most creditor claims must be filed within three months of the first published notice, and the estate usually stays open until then. A sale can often close during that period; the attorney says how the proceeds are held meanwhile.
  7. Protect the house while you wait. Keep the insurance active (an empty house may need a vacancy policy), and keep the utilities, lawn and property taxes up to date.

Common questions

Inherited a house in Seminole County: common questions

Where is probate filed for a home in Seminole County?

In the county where the person lived when they passed away. For Seminole County that is the Seminole County Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller, part of the Eighteenth Judicial Circuit. Florida has no separate probate court; probate is a division of the circuit court.

How do I find out who owns a Seminole County home and whether it has homestead?

The Seminole County Property Appraiser website shows the owner of record, the homestead status and the last recorded transfer. The clerk's official records show the recorded deed and any mortgage or lien.

Can the home be sold before the probate case is finished?

Often, once the court has appointed a personal representative, and sometimes with a court order for the sale. If the home passed outside probate, probate may not be needed. The family's attorney confirms which applies.

Do I need court approval to sell a probate house in Seminole County?

Not always. If the will gives the personal representative a power to sell real property, the personal representative can usually sell without a court order. With no will, or no usable power of sale, the sale needs the court to authorize or confirm it before title passes. A protected homestead follows a different path. The estate’s attorney confirms which applies.

How long does probate take before a Seminole County house can be sold?

Summary administration often takes a few weeks to a few months. Formal administration more often takes six to twelve months in total, but the house can often be listed once a personal representative is appointed, well before the case closes. The family's attorney confirms the timing.

Can a family member who lives out of state handle this?

Florida limits who can serve as a personal representative when they live out of state. The attorney confirms who qualifies. Either way, someone local can check the home, collect mail and keep the utilities on while the court process runs. How we help families who live out of state.
