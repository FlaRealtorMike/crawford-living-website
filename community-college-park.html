Communities

College Park

One of Orlando's original neighborhoods: bungalows, big shade trees, a lake you can walk around, and a main street of locally owned restaurants.

Orientation

College Park is inside the City of Orlando, a short drive northwest of downtown. It took shape in the 1920s, and many of its streets are named after colleges and universities.

The homes

Expect three eras: early twentieth-century bungalows and cottages, mid-century ranch homes, and newer homes built where older ones once stood. Lots are usually modest; the tree cover is a big part of the charm. Many streets have no mandatory HOA. Condition ranges from original to fully renovated, and that drives both price and budget.

Daily life

Edgewater Drive is the main street, with independent restaurants, coffee shops and small shops. Lake Adair, about 24 acres, has a trail along its edge and a park. There's a public golf course that opened in 1924, and parks and playgrounds on the side streets.

Getting around

Downtown Orlando is a short drive or bike ride away, and I-4 is easy to reach. Parking and traffic near Edgewater can get busy on weekends.

Before you buy here

With older homes, look hard at the foundation, roof, electrical, plumbing and the age of the air conditioning. A four-point inspection is worth having — insurers often want one. On a renovated home, ask for the permits. If you plan to renovate, ask the city what's required first. School zones are set by address — confirm with Orange County Public Schools.

Price ranges

Original-condition bungalows from about $400K. Updated homes about $550K to $900K. Lakefront $1M+.
