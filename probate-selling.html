How a sale happens

In order, with the attorney first

  1. 01Authority is confirmedYour attorney confirms who must sign, and whether the court has to authorize or confirm the sale.
  2. 02Paperwork reaches title earlyThe title company will want the Letters of Administration and, once there is a buyer, a deed signed by the personal representative, well before the closing table.
  3. 03The ordinary workPricing, whether to prepare the home or sell it as-is, the belongings, access and showings, disclosures, and a schedule that fits where the estate stands.
  4. 04ClosingA sale that closes early may still need to hold its proceeds until the estate’s debts are resolved.
Where we fit in

What a broker can do, and when

Our part can begin well before the family has decided anything, once your attorney has cleared who may authorize access and decisions. The aim is not to move anyone toward a sale.

  • Look at the home honestly: condition, access, and how much needs clearing first.
  • Build a current market picture from recent nearby sales and the active competition.
  • Compare the options with real numbers: as-is against repaired, and the work each takes.
  • Build the plan once a direction is chosen, coordinated with your attorney and the title company.
  • Help when the family doesn’t agree, which is often the hardest part.

The boundary: we don’t give legal, tax, or financial advice. Those questions stay with your attorney and the other professionals. Our part is the home, the market, and the practical work each option takes.
