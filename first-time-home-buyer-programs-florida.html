First-time buyers

First-Time Buyer Programs in Florida

The biggest hurdle is often the cash up front. There is real help for the down payment and closing costs; it just isn't always easy to find.

Video

Prefer YouTube? Watch it there.

The short version

  • Help usually comes from four places: the state through Florida Housing and participating lenders, your county or city, some lenders, and some employers.
  • Most assistance is a second loan: deferred with no payment, forgiven a little each year, or a small monthly payment. Know which one you're signing.
  • Common rules: first-time buyer (often no owned home in three years), income and price limits, a minimum credit score, a homebuyer education class, and a participating lender.
  • Florida's Hometown Heroes program helps people who serve their communities. Its funding opens and closes, so check when you're ready.
  • Orange County and the City of Orlando have offered help too; local funds can pause when money runs out. Start early.

What to do next

  1. Talk with a lender who participates in Florida Housing programs and ask which ones you qualify for today.
  2. Take the homebuyer education class early.
  3. Read how each program is repaid so there are no surprises when you sell or refinance.

Questions

Who counts as a first-time home buyer?

Many programs count anyone who hasn't owned a home they lived in during the past three years. Check each program's rules.

Do I have to pay back down payment assistance?

Usually it's a second loan. Some have no payment or interest until you sell, refinance or move; some are forgiven over time; some have a small monthly payment.

What is Florida Hometown Heroes?

A state program for people who serve their communities, such as health care workers, school staff, first responders, child care workers, and many military members and veterans. Its funding opens and closes.
