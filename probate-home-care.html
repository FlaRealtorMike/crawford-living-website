Insurance

Often the first call

A death, a change in occupancy, or a long vacancy can change what a policy covers, and some policies stop covering a home once it sits empty.

The point is to confirm with the insurance agent what is actually in place, not to assume the existing coverage carries on unchanged.

Steady upkeep

An empty house develops problems quietly

After insurance, it is mostly steadiness:

  • Access and locks, so you know who can get in.
  • Utilities kept on where they protect the home.
  • Regular checks for leaks or storm damage.
  • The lawn and the mail, so the house looks looked after.

Responsible for the home from out of state? See help for out-of-state owners.

The belongings

Before anything leaves the house

The belongings inside are part of the estate too. Before anything is removed, donated, or discarded, an attorney can confirm who has the authority and whether the will affects the contents.

Photographs and a simple inventory are a good place to start.

Protecting the home is less about making it perfect than about preventing avoidable loss while the estate decides what happens next.
