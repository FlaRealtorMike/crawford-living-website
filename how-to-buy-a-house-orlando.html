Buying a home

How to Buy a House in Orlando, Step by Step

It comes down to six steps, in order. Once you know what's coming, it gets a lot less stressful.

Prefer YouTube? Watch it there.

The short version

  • 1. Budget: get pre-approved and decide on a monthly payment you're comfortable with, taxes, insurance and HOA included.
  • 2. Choose an agent: you'll sign a written buyer agreement before touring. Pay is negotiable, and sellers sometimes cover some or all of it.
  • 3. Search: list must-haves, and on any home you love, check the HOA, any CDD, the flood zone, and an insurance quote.
  • 4. Offer: price, escrow deposit (held by the title company), financing, inspection days and closing date.
  • 5. Under contract: inspections, appraisal, insurance and underwriting. Don't change jobs or open new credit until you close.
  • 6. Closing: final walkthrough, signing and keys. Confirm wiring instructions by phone at a number you already know. File for homestead by March 1.

What to do next

  1. Get pre-approved before you tour.
  2. Read your buyer agreement and ask questions before you sign.
  3. Never wire money without confirming instructions by phone with the title company.

Questions

Do I have to sign an agreement with a buyer's agent in Florida?

Yes. Before touring homes with an agent you'll sign a written agreement that spells out what the agent does and how they're paid. That pay is negotiable.

Who holds the escrow deposit?

Typically the title company holds the buyer's escrow deposit.

How do I avoid wire fraud when closing?

Before sending any money, call the title company at a number you already know is real and confirm the wiring instructions by phone.

Buying a home can feel like a maze, especially the first time. But it really comes down to six steps, in order. Once you know what's coming, it gets a lot less stressful. Let's walk through the whole thing, start to finish, the way it works here in Central Florida. Step 1 is your budget.

Before you look at a single house, talk with a lender and get pre-approved. That tells you what you can borrow. But just as important is deciding what monthly payment you're comfortable with with taxes, insurance, and any HOA included. I made a whole video on this (see How much house can I afford?). Step 2 is choosing an agent.

These days, before you tour homes with an agent, you'll sign a written agreement. It spells out what the agent does for you and how they're paid. That pay is negotiable, and sometimes the seller agrees to cover some, or all of it. Read the agreement, ask questions, and make sure you're comfortable before you sign. Step 3 is the fun part.

Make a short list of must-haves and nice-to-haves, so you can decide quickly. And, in Florida, check a few things early on any home you love. The HOA rules and fees. Whether there's a CDD, the flood zone, and an insurance quote, those can change the payment more than the price does. Step 4 is making an offer.

Your offer covers more than price. It includes your escrow deposit, which is held by the title company, your financing, how many days you'll have for inspections, and your closing date. Florida has a couple of widely used contract forms, and your agent will walk you through which one fits. Step 5 is the busy stretch after your offer is accepted. You'll do your inspections, the lender orders an appraisal, you'll lock in homeowners insurance, and the loan goes through underwriting.

One tip. During this time, don't change jobs, open new credit cards, or finance a big purchase. Keep your finances as steady as possible until you close. Step 6 is Closing Day. You'll do a final walkthrough to make sure the home is in the condition you agreed to.

Then you sign, the funds move, and you get the keys. One important tip, wire fraud is real. Before you send any money, call the title company at a number you already know is real and confirm the instructions by phone. And after you move in, file for your Homestead Exemption by March 1st.
