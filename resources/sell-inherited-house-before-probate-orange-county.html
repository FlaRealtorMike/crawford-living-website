Can You Sell an Inherited House Before Probate Is Finished in Orange County, Florida?

Often, yes. Florida does not make a family wait until the probate case closes. You need someone with legal authority to sign the deed.

By Michael Crawford, Broker, Crawford Living Realty, LLC
Published November 20, 2026

The short answer

Often, yes. Florida does not make a family wait until the probate case closes to sell a house. What you need is someone with legal authority to sign the deed. Who that person is depends on how the house was owned, whether there is a will, and whether the house was the owner's homestead.

Here is how that plays out when the house is in Orange County.

Find out how the house was titled

Start with the deed. A house titled in the owner's name alone, or with another person as tenants in common, is a probate asset, unless it was the owner's homestead (more on that below). A house owned jointly "with rights of survivorship," or by a married couple as "tenants by the entirety," passes to the surviving owner without probate 1. A house that was deeded into a trust before the owner passed away generally avoids probate, and the successor trustee can act without a court appointment 14. You can look up the parcel on the Orange County Property Appraiser's site 13 and the recorded deed in the Orange County Comptroller's Official Records 15. Your probate attorney will confirm which path applies.

Open the probate case with the Orange County Clerk

Orange County is part of Florida's Ninth Judicial Circuit, which also covers Osceola County 3. Probate cases here are filed with the Orange County Clerk of Courts, Probate Division, 425 N. Orange Avenue, Orlando, either in person, by mail, or through the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal 4.

There are two main paths:

  • Summary administration is available when the estate subject to administration in Florida, less exempt property, is $150,000 or less, or when the owner passed away more than two years ago 5. The Clerk's filing fee is $345 for estates over $1,000 ($235 for $1,000 or less) 6. Here the court's order assigns the property to the people entitled to it 16.
  • Formal administration is the full, court-supervised process. It is filed by a Florida-licensed attorney (the Orange County Clerk notes this requirement), and the Clerk's filing fee is $400 46.

Wait for the personal representative to be appointed

In formal administration, the court appoints a personal representative (Florida's term for an executor). That person's powers begin on appointment 7, and the Clerk issues Letters of Administration as proof of that authority 4. Until then, no one has authority to sign a deed for the estate, and a title company will not close without the letters.

Check the will for a "power of sale"

This one detail decides whether you need a judge's sign-off:

  • The will gives a power of sale. If the will grants a specific power to sell real property, or a general power to sell any estate asset, the personal representative can sell without court authorization or confirmation 8.
  • No will, or no usable power of sale. The personal representative can still sell, but no title passes until the court authorizes or confirms the sale 8. In practice, the listing and contract should allow time for that order.

When the sale is made under a specific power in the will to sell real property, or under a court order authorizing or confirming it, the buyer takes title free of estate creditors' claims and beneficiaries' entitlements. Existing mortgages and other liens are not affected 8, so they still have to be satisfied, usually out of the sale proceeds.

If it was the owner's homestead, the rules change

Florida treats a "protected homestead" differently. It is not one of the assets the personal representative controls, although the personal representative may step in to insure and protect it 9. Instead, it passes to the people entitled to it under Florida law, and those people generally sign the deed (your attorney will confirm who). To document who they are, an interested person can ask the court for an order determining protected homestead status. That order identifies by name the people entitled to the property and each person's share 10. In Orange County's probate divisions, the Clerk audits homestead orders before the judge reviews them, which can add time 11.

The creditor period does not have to hold up the sale

In formal administration, most creditor claims must be filed within three months after the first publication of the notice to creditors, or 30 days after service for creditors who must be served, if later 12. The estate generally won't be closed until that period has run. Florida's sale statute does not make a sale wait for the claims period to end 8, but open claims, the will's terms or the title insurer can affect timing. Your attorney will tell you how sale proceeds should be handled while claims are still open.

While you wait, protect the house

Keep the insurance active, keep the lawn and utilities going, and keep paying the property taxes. Also tell the Orange County Property Appraiser about the change in ownership. A change in title requires a new homestead application, the Save Our Homes limit expires with a change in ownership, and the Property Appraiser asks owners to report any change in ownership or use 217.

A different way to sell an inherited home

Most families selling an inherited house are juggling grief, siblings, an attorney, and a property that may have sat empty for months. A probate sale goes smoother when the agent works alongside the estate attorney from day one: pricing from recent comparable sales, writing the contract so it allows for court confirmation when needed, and keeping every heir informed so nobody is surprised at the closing table.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my parent's house in Orange County before probate is closed?

Often yes, once someone has legal authority to sign: a personal representative with a power of sale or a court order, or, for a protected homestead, the people the court identifies as entitled to it 810.

Do I need court approval to sell a probate house in Florida?

Generally, when the personal representative is selling without a usable power of sale in the will. In that case no title passes until the court authorizes or confirms the sale 8. Protected homestead and summary administration follow different paths.

Where are probate cases filed in Orange County?

With the Orange County Clerk of Courts, Probate Division, at 425 N. Orange Avenue in Orlando, or through the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal 4.
