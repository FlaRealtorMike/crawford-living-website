Selling a home

What It Really Costs to Sell a Home in Florida

The sale price is the headline. What matters is what you walk away with. Here are the real costs of selling in Florida so there are no surprises at closing.

Video

Prefer YouTube? Watch it there.

The short version

  • Commission is usually the biggest piece and has always been negotiable. Since 2024, buyers sign their own agreements; whether to help with the buyer's side is your decision.
  • Florida charges documentary stamp tax when a deed is recorded; in most of Florida the seller customarily pays it.
  • In Orange, Seminole and Osceola counties, the seller customarily pays for the buyer's owner's title insurance policy. Florida title rates are set by the state.
  • Payoffs: your mortgage and any home equity line, a property tax proration credit to the buyer, HOA payoff letter fees, and any liens or open permits.
  • Before and after listing: small repairs, paint, cleaning, staging, possible repair credits after the buyer's inspection, and moving.

A $400,000 example

Start with the $400,000 price. Subtract your mortgage payoff and the commission you've agreed to. Subtract the documentary stamp tax (about $2,800) and the owner's title policy (about $2,000) — about $4,800 together. Then subtract smaller items: title fees, the tax proration, HOA fees and any repair credits. What's left is your net.

What to do next

  1. Ask for a written seller net sheet based on your actual mortgage balance and a realistic price before you sign a listing agreement.
  2. Agree on how commission and any help with the buyer's side will be handled.
  3. Gather your HOA contact and latest tax bill so nothing slows down closing.

Questions

Who pays documentary stamp tax on a Florida home sale?

In most of Florida, the seller customarily pays the documentary stamp tax on the deed. On a $400,000 sale it's about $2,800.

Who pays for title insurance in Orlando?

In Orange, Seminole and Osceola counties, the seller customarily pays for the buyer's owner's title insurance policy. Florida title insurance rates are set by the state.

Is real estate commission negotiable in Florida?

Yes, it has always been negotiable. Since the 2024 changes, buyers sign their own agreements with their agents, and whether a seller helps with the buyer's side is a decision made with the listing agent.
