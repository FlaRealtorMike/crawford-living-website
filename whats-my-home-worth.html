Selling a home

What's My Home Really Worth?

An online estimate is a starting point, not a price. Here is what really decides your home's value and how to get a number you can plan around.

Video

Prefer YouTube? Watch it there.

The short version

  • Online estimates use public records and nearby sales. They can't see your updates, your roof's age, or a busy road behind the house.
  • Value comes down to recent comparable sales, the homes you'd compete with now, condition and updates, and location details.
  • Three numbers: the online estimate (an automated guess), the county assessed value (for taxes, often below market in Florida), and a market valuation or appraisal (the one to plan around).
  • Central Florida buyers look closely at roof age, A/C age and hurricane features, partly because of insurance.
  • A new listing gets the most attention in its first couple of weeks. Overpricing often leads to price cuts and a lower result.

What to do next

  1. Note your roof and A/C years, upgrades and HOA details.
  2. Get a written market valuation from a local agent who walks through the home.
  3. Plan fresh paint, a deep clean and small repairs rather than a big remodel.

Questions

Why is my Zillow estimate wrong?

Automated estimates can't see inside your home or know about updates, roof age or lot details, so they can miss in either direction.

Is my county assessed value my market value?

No. In Florida, assessed value is for property taxes and is often well below market value because of homestead caps.

Should I remodel before selling?

Fresh paint, a deep clean and small repairs usually pay off. Big remodels right before selling rarely return what they cost.

If you've ever typed your address into a home value website, you've probably gotten a number. Maybe you liked it, maybe you didn't. Here's the thing, that number is a starting point, not a price. Let's talk about what really decides what your home is worth and how to get a number you can actually plan around. Online estimates are built by computers looking at public records and nearby sales.

What they can't see is the inside of your home. They don't know your kitchen was redone, or that the roof is two years old, or that the house backs up to a busy road. That's why the estimate can be off, sometimes in your favor and sometimes not. In the real world, value comes down to four things. Recent sales of similar homes nearby, especially in the last few months.

The homes you'd be competing with right now. The condition and updates of your home. And the location details, like the street, the lot, and the view. A buyer will compare your home to the others they can buy today, and that comparison sets the price. You'll run into three different numbers, and they mean different things.

The online estimate is an automated guess. Your county assessed value is for property taxes, and in Florida it's often well below market value because of homestead caps. And a market valuation from a local agent, or an appraisal ordered by a lender, looks at your actual home and the actual comparable sales. That last one is the number to plan around. In central Florida, buyers pay close attention to a few things, partly because of insurance.

The age of the roof. The age of the air conditioning. Hurricane features, like impact windows or shutters. Those can matter more than a new backsplash. Fresh paint, a deep clean, and small repairs usually pay off.

Big remodels right before selling rarely return what they cost. The price you start at matters more than most people think. A new listing gets the most attention in its first couple of weeks. If it's priced too high, buyers skip it. It sits.

And price cuts follow, which can end with a lower sale than if it was priced right from day one. Pricing well isn't about leaving money on the table. It's about getting the right buyers to show up. So here's what I'd do. Jot down what you know about your home the year of the roof and the air conditioning, any upgrades, and your HOA details if you have one.

Then get a written market valuation from a local agent who walks through the home. You'll get a real range, and a plan to go with it.
