Buying a home

Home Inspections in Florida

Once your offer is accepted, the inspection is one of the most important steps. Florida has a few extra inspections most people haven't heard of until they need them.

The short version

  • A general inspection is a visual check of the roof, structure, electrical, plumbing, air conditioning, water heater, attic, windows and doors. It isn't pass/fail.
  • Florida add-ons: a 4-point (often required by insurers on older homes), wind mitigation (can lower premiums), a WDO/termite inspection, and a pool inspection.
  • In Florida's widely used as-is contract, you can cancel for any reason during the inspection window. Schedule inspections right away.
  • Focus the report on the roof, structure, water intrusion and big systems, then safety items. Cosmetic items come with owning a home.
  • Options if something big turns up: ask for a repair, a credit, a lower price, or walk away in your window. Get an insurance quote during the window.

What to do next

  1. Book the general inspection and Florida add-ons as soon as the contract is signed.
  2. Read the report for the big four: roof, structure, water and major systems.
  3. Get an insurance quote before the inspection window closes.

Questions

What is a 4-point inspection?

An inspection of the roof, electrical, plumbing, and heating and cooling. Insurance companies often require one on older homes.

What is a wind mitigation inspection?

It documents features such as roof straps and hurricane shutters, and it can lower your homeowners insurance premium.

Can I cancel a Florida as-is contract after the inspection?

Under Florida's widely used as-is contract, a buyer can cancel for any reason during the inspection period. Don't let the window run out.

In Florida, there are a few extra inspections most people have never heard of until they need them. Let's walk through what gets checked, what's extra, and what to do with the report. A general home inspection is a visual check of the home's major parts.

The roof, the structure, the electrical system, the plumbing, the air conditioning, the water heater, the attic, and the windows and doors. The inspector isn't opening walls, and it isn't a pass or fail test. Think of it as a detailed health check so you know what you're buying. Here in Florida, a few add-ons come up a lot. A four-point inspection looks at the roof, electrical, plumbing, and heating and cooling, and insurance companies often ask for one on older homes.

A wind mitigation inspection documents features like roof straps and hurricane shutters, and it can lower your insurance premium. A termite inspection, sometimes called a WDO, checks for wood-destroying insects. And if there's a pool, it's worth having it looked at too. Your contract gives you a set number of days to do all of this, so schedule inspections right away. In Florida's widely used as-is contract, you can cancel for any reason during that inspection window.

That makes the window valuable. Don't let it run out while you're waiting on a quote. Every report has findings, even on new homes. Don't panic at the page count. Focus on four things.

The roof, the structure, any signs of water getting in, and the big systems like air conditioning and electrical. Safety issues, come next. Cosmetic items, like a loose cabinet door, are just the cost of owning a home. If something big turns up, you have options. You can ask the seller to fix it, ask for a credit at closing, ask for a lower price, or walk away during your window.

Credits are often cleaner than repairs, because you control who does the work. And in an as-is contract, the seller doesn't have to agree to any of it, so the conversation matters. One Florida-specific note about roofs. Insurance companies look closely at roof age. Florida law gives homeowners some protection for younger roofs, and an older roof can still be insurable if an inspection shows it has enough life left.
