Inherited homes · Orlando and Central Florida

You inherited a house. Here is what to do first.

Nobody plans for this part. There's a home in Orlando, or Oviedo, or Kissimmee, that belonged to a parent or a relative, and now it is partly your responsibility, along with everything else. This page is the practical version: what to protect, who can sign, and how the Central Florida courts and counties fit in. It is general information, not legal advice.

The first week

The first week: protect the house, then slow down

Most of what goes wrong with an inherited home happens in the first month, and almost none of it is about the sale. It's a burst pipe nobody noticed, a lapsed insurance policy, or a family member who moved furniture out before anyone agreed who it belonged to.

  • 1. Secure it

    Who has keys? Change or re-key the locks if you're not sure. Check the doors, windows, garage, and that the alarm, if there is one, still has a working account.

  • 2. Keep the utilities on

    Power for the air conditioning, water off at the main if the house will sit empty, and the irrigation on a timer so the yard doesn't announce that nobody's home.

  • 3. Call the insurance company

    Tell them the owner has died and the home is vacant. Many policies limit coverage on a vacant house after a set number of days; a vacant-home endorsement is usually available.

  • 4. Collect the mail and the paperwork

    Forward the mail. Look for the deed, the mortgage statement, the property tax bill, the HOA, and any homeowner's insurance. These answer the first questions an attorney will ask.

  • 5. Don't move things out yet

    Photograph each room as it is. Until someone has legal authority over the estate, the contents aren't anyone's to distribute, and photographs settle a lot of later disagreements.

  • 6. Find out how the house was titled

    This single fact decides whether probate is needed at all. The county property appraiser's website shows the owner of record; your attorney reads the deed.

Probate, or not

Probate, or not: it depends on the deed

People search “inherited house” far more often than “probate,” and the reason is that most families don't yet know whether probate applies to them. The short version:

  • Home in a trust

    The successor trustee usually has authority to sell without probate.

  • Lady bird deed (enhanced life estate)

    The home passes to the named person at death, generally without probate.

  • Joint owners with right of survivorship, or spouses

    The surviving owner typically takes title by recording a death certificate.

  • Titled only in the person who died, with or without a will

    This is the case that goes through probate, in the circuit court of the county where they lived.

Florida has a shorter form of probate (summary administration) for smaller estates and older deaths, and a longer one (formal administration) where the court appoints a personal representative. Which one applies, and how long it takes, is your attorney's call. Our probate and estates guide walks through the timeline, homestead, and how a sale happens step by step.

Where it's filed

Where the case is filed in Central Florida

Probate is filed in the county where the person lived, and the five counties around Orlando sit in four different judicial circuits. Each clerk has its own probate department and its own way of doing things.

What it's worth

What the house is worth, and why that's two different questions

The estate usually needs a value as of the date of death, for the inventory and for the heirs' tax basis. That is a question for an appraiser. What the house would sell for today, in its current condition, with the market as it is, is a market valuation, and that one I can give you, in writing, after walking the home.

They are often different numbers, and both matter. The first protects the heirs later. The second helps the family decide what to do now.

Keep, rent, or sell

Keep it, rent it, or sell it: deciding on purpose

There is no right answer, only the right answer for this family. A few things that tend to tip it:

  • How many heirs there are, and where they live

    Three siblings in three states rarely want to co-own a rental in Orlando.

  • The mortgage

    A loan doesn't disappear at death; the payments continue, and heirs generally have the right to keep paying it.

  • The tax bill

    The homestead exemption and the Save Our Homes cap end with the owner; next year's taxes on an inherited home can be considerably higher.

  • The condition

    A home that hasn't been updated in decades can still sell well, but the choice between fixing and selling as-is should be made with numbers, not guilt.

If the answer is to sell, the way we sell a home starts with an honest first number, a plain read on what's worth fixing, and clear updates to every heir while it's listed, not only to the one who signed.

Common questions

Inherited a house in Orlando: common questions

Can I sell my parents' house before probate is finished?

Often, yes, once the court has appointed a personal representative, and sometimes with a court order for the sale. If the home passed outside probate, probate may not be needed at all. Your attorney confirms which applies.

Do I have to go through probate at all?

It depends on how the home was titled. Trusts, lady bird deeds, and joint ownership with survivorship usually avoid it. A home titled only in the person who died generally does not.

Who can sign the listing agreement?

The person with legal authority: the court-appointed personal representative, the successor trustee, or the heirs once title is in their names. I can help you prepare before that point, but can't list the home until someone has that authority.

What happens to the homestead exemption?

It ended with the person who lived there. The assessed value usually resets, so the next bill can be higher. An heir who moves in and qualifies applies for their own.

Should we empty the house before selling?

Usually yes, but not before you have the authority to deal with the contents and have checked with the attorney. Photograph everything first, keep the paperwork, and give the family time.

Is a cash offer from an investor a good idea?

Sometimes. It is fast and simple, and it is almost always below what the home would bring on the open market. The right move is to know the market number first, then decide whether speed is worth the difference.
