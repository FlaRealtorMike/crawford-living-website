For probate attorneys

The legal file stays yours. The house is the part I handle.

When a probate matter includes a home, most of what it creates isn't legal work. It's the house: cleared, repaired, priced, and carried to closing. That's real estate and construction coordination, and I've done it for twenty years.

I'm Mike Crawford, a Florida-licensed real estate broker since 2004. I took the license while I was in the construction business, at a company building roughly 400 homes a year — no small operation. My role was coordinating the licensed contractors who did the work, rather than doing it myself. An estate's home is often one that needs work before it can sell, and that coordination is what gets it there.

The legal and procedural side of an estate is yours: authority to sell, homestead, court approval, the creditor period. I route every one of those back to you and wait for your lead. What's left is the house, and a family that doesn't always agree about it. That's the work I take on.

A probate sale isn't a standard closing

  • Authority first

    A home doesn't go to market until the authority to sign for it is established. That's yours to determine; I wait for it.

  • Title, early

    I learn what the title company will need for an estate sale up front, and raise anything unusual with you and title early — not the week before closing.

  • A value as of the date of death

    If the estate needs one, I provide a broker's opinion of value as of the date of death, at no cost.

  • The things that catch families out

    I flag the practical risks of an empty house — like a homeowner's policy that can stop covering a home once it sits vacant.

What I don't do

I don't give legal advice, and I don't assume authority to sell before you've established it. No referral fees change hands, in either direction — just good work sent to good people.

This is a professional introduction only — not a solicitation for legal referral, and not an offer of compensation for referrals, ever. Crawford Living does not give legal or tax advice.
