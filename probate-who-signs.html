Authority

Who is allowed to act?

A family often knows what it hopes to do with a home long before anyone is allowed to carry that plan out. In Florida, the court appoints a personal representative and issues Letters of Administration. Until that authority exists, major decisions about the home are premature.

If there is a will, it usually names an executor. If there is no will, the court appoints an administrator. Florida calls either one the personal representative.

How the home was titled

Title decides the path

In one name only
Usually needs probate. The court-appointed personal representative signs.
In a living trust
Can often pass outside probate. The successor trustee signs.
Joint, or a lady bird deed
Joint ownership with survivorship, or a lady bird deed, often passes the home without probate.

If you don’t yet have a probate attorney, that is the first call to make, before any decision about the home. We’re glad to point you toward a probate attorney we trust in Central Florida, with no arrangement of any kind between us.

How long it takes

Two paths through Florida probate

Summary administration

A few weeks to a few months

For smaller estates, or when the person passed away more than two years ago. It skips several formal steps, and often the creditor waiting period.

Formal administration

More often six to twelve months

The full court-supervised process, with a personal representative appointed and a required waiting period for creditors before the estate can close.

  1. 01First weeks: you’re appointedThe court issues Letters of Administration. Until they exist, no one can sign to sell the home.
  2. 02Months 1–3: the creditor periodA set window lets creditors come forward. No court shortens it, and most of the waiting is that window running its course.
  3. 03Months 3–6: debts, then distributionValid claims are addressed first. A sale that closes earlier may hold its proceeds until this step is complete.
  4. 04Around a year: the estate closesThe attorney prepares the final accounting. Some estates take longer.

A general sequence, not a schedule. Florida’s 67 counties each move at their own pace, and which path applies is a question for your attorney.

Homestead

Could the home be protected homestead?

In Florida, homestead can carry legal weight well beyond the property-tax exemption most people know. It can affect who receives the home, how the will applies, and who may make decisions about it.

The practical point is not to work it out yourself. It is to know the question exists, and to raise it with your attorney before the family settles on a plan.

Words you’ll hear

The terms, in plain language

Personal representative
The person with court authority to act for the estate. An executor or an administrator; Florida uses one term for both.
Letters of Administration
The court document that proves the personal representative can act. A title company will ask to see it before a sale.
Creditor period
A set window during which the estate’s creditors can come forward. The main reason formal probate can’t be hurried.
Protected homestead
A legal status a Florida home may carry that can change who receives it and who may decide about it.
Date-of-death value
What the home was worth on the day the owner passed away. Different from today’s market value.
Comparative market analysis
A broker’s read on what a home would sell for now, from recent nearby sales. Not a formal appraisal.
Personal representative’s deed
The deed the personal representative signs to transfer the home when the estate sells it.
Final accounting
The attorney’s summary of what came in and went out, filed as the estate closes.
