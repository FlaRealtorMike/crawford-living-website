Buying a home

How Much House Can I Afford in Orlando?

Start with the monthly payment you can live with, not the price on the listing. Here is the simple math, the Central Florida costs that surprise buyers, and a worked example.

Video

Prefer YouTube? Watch it there.

The short version

  • Plan around the full monthly payment, including taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance and any HOA, not the list price.
  • The 28/36 rule: keep the housing payment near 28% of gross monthly income, and all monthly debts near 36%. Treat it as a comfortable lane, not a ceiling.
  • Don't budget from the seller's tax bill. In Florida, taxes are reset on the new value after a sale. File for homestead on a primary home by March 1.
  • Get a homeowners insurance quote early. Roof age matters a lot in Florida.
  • Ask whether a home has a CDD (Community Development District). The fee appears on the property tax bill, so it is easy to miss.

A worked example

A household earning $100,000 a year makes about $8,333 a month. 28% of that is about $2,333 for the full housing payment.

Take out estimated taxes (about $400), insurance ($250), mortgage insurance ($100) and an HOA ($100), and roughly $1,483 is left for principal and interest. At 6.5% on a 30-year loan, that supports a loan of about $235,000 — with 10% down, a home around $260,000.

The 36% check: $3,000 a month for all debts leaves about $667 after the house payment, so a $400 car payment still fits. Plan for closing costs of a few percent on top of the down payment.

Illustration only. Your rate, down payment, taxes and fees will change the answer.

What to do next

  1. Decide the monthly payment you are truly comfortable with.
  2. Talk with a lender and get pre-approved so you know your real rate and loan options.
  3. Get insurance quotes and ask about taxes, HOA and CDD fees on any home you are serious about.

Questions

What is the 28/36 rule?

Lenders often check that your total housing payment stays at or under about 28% of your gross monthly income, and that all monthly debts, including the house, stay at or under about 36%. Some loan programs allow more, but those numbers are a comfortable lane.

Why might my property taxes be higher than the seller's?

In Florida, a long-time owner's taxable value can be capped well below market value. After you buy, the home is reassessed at its new value, so your bill can be noticeably higher. Estimate taxes on your purchase price.

What is a CDD fee?

Many newer Orlando-area communities have a Community Development District. Its fee is collected on the property tax bill rather than as an HOA charge, so always ask whether a home has one.
