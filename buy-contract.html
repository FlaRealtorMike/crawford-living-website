The deadlines that matter

Each date is leverage while it’s open.

Once your offer is accepted, the contract sets dated stretches of time to inspect, verify, renegotiate, or step away without losing your deposit. Once a date passes, that right is generally gone.

  1. 01Offer acceptedYour deposit goes into escrow, and the contract’s dates start counting.
  2. 02The inspection periodYour time to examine the house and, if something turns up, renegotiate or walk away with your deposit. It’s the most important deadline in the contract, and it doesn’t reopen.
  3. 03Financing and appraisalYour loan is underwritten and the home is appraised. Contingencies here protect you if the financing or the value doesn’t come together.
  4. 04Final walkthroughOne last look to confirm the house is in the condition you agreed to, before you sign.
  5. 05ClosingDocuments signed, funds moved, and the home is yours.

The exact counts are written into your contract, and Florida’s standard forms set them. Between me and the transaction coordinator on your file, none of them slips.

Negotiation

A deal you’d make again.

Price is one part of it. The rest is the terms: what gets fixed before closing, what credits change hands, and how much room the dates give you.

I’m a certified Real Estate Negotiation Expert (RENE). Much of the work is keeping a deal calm and moving when it would otherwise stall over something small.

To closing

Fewer surprises between contract and keys.

Most of what goes wrong is a date missed, a document late, or a question raised too close to the end. The transaction coordinator keeps the file moving. My part is catching the judgment calls early.

If something comes up, you’ll hear it from me early, with what it means and your options, not the afternoon before closing.
