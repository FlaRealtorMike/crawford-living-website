Where it's filed

Where probate is filed in Orange County

Probate is filed in the county where the person lived. For Orange County, that is the Orange County Clerk of Courts, Probate Division, in the Ninth Judicial Circuit. The clerk's probate office is at 425 N. Orange Avenue, Suite 335, in downtown Orlando. The phone is 407-836-2057.

The clerk's online case search shows the case once it is filed, including the filings and the date each was entered.

An attorney files the case for most families. If there is no attorney yet, the first call is to a Florida probate attorney, not to a real estate agent. Once there is one, I work to the attorney's timeline.

Who owns it

How to look up the owner, the homestead and the deed

The Orange County Property Appraiser website shows the owner of record, whether the home has a homestead exemption, and the last recorded transfer. The clerk's official records show the recorded deed, any mortgage and any lien. Together they answer the first question a family has: whose name is on the home, and does it need probate to move?

A home held in a trust, under a lady bird deed, or jointly with survivorship often passes without a probate case. A home titled only in the name of the person who died generally does not. The Central Florida inherited-house guide walks through each case.

What tends to matter for homes in Orange County

Many Orange County homes sit inside a homeowners' association, and some sit inside a community development district as well. Both can charge fees, and an association will usually be asked for a statement of what is owed before a sale closes. That request goes faster when someone starts it early, so it belongs on the list as soon as the family knows the home will be sold.

Common questions

Inherited a house in Orange County: common questions

Where is probate filed for a home in Orange County?

In the county where the person lived when they died. For Orange County that is the Orange County Clerk of Courts, Probate Division, part of the Ninth Judicial Circuit. Florida has no separate probate court; probate is a division of the circuit court.

How do I find out who owns an Orange County home and whether it has homestead?

The Orange County Property Appraiser website shows the owner of record, the homestead status and the last recorded transfer. The clerk's official records show the recorded deed and any mortgage or lien.

Can the home be sold before the probate case is finished?

Often, once the court has appointed a personal representative, and sometimes with a court order for the sale. If the home passed outside probate, probate may not be needed. The family's attorney confirms which applies.

Can a family member who lives out of state handle this?

Florida limits who can serve as a personal representative when they live out of state. The attorney confirms who qualifies. Either way, someone local can check the home, collect mail and keep the utilities on while the court process runs.
