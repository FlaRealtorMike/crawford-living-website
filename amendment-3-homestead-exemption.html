Florida Property Taxes

Amendment 3 Passed: Florida’s New Homestead Exemption

On November 3, 2026, Florida voters approved a much larger homestead exemption. Here is what changes, when, who qualifies, and what to do before the March 1 filing deadline.

The short version

Amendment 3 passed with about 62% of the vote (unofficial results; Florida amendments need 60%). Starting January 1, 2027, a Florida homestead gets a much bigger break on the non-school part of the property tax bill. The school-tax portion doesn’t change.

2026 (today)
Up to $50,000 — $25,000 off all property taxes, plus a second $25,000 off non-school taxes
2027
Up to $150,000 off non-school property taxes
2028 and after
Up to $250,000 off non-school property taxes, adjusted for inflation each year after that
School taxes
The $25,000 exemption stays the same
Non-homestead property
The yearly cap on assessment increases drops from 10% to 5%

Will my taxes go down?

Not necessarily. A larger exemption lowers the taxable value of your home. Your bill is that taxable value times the millage rates your city, county and other local taxing districts set each year — and those rates can change. Most homesteads should see a lower taxable value; what the final bill does depends on both.

New to Florida?

If you are not a Florida resident on December 31, 2026, you receive the existing homestead exemption first. The increased exemption applies starting in your fifth year of having the exemption.

If you’re relocating, this is worth factoring into a monthly budget now, rather than assuming the full amount on day one.

How to file

  1. Own the home and live in it as your permanent residence on January 1.
  2. File with your county property appraiser by March 1. Most counties take it online.
  3. Filing is free. You don’t need to pay anyone to do it for you.

Already have homestead? It renews each year on its own. Watch for notices from your county property appraiser on how the increase is applied.

We work in Orange, Seminole, Osceola, Lake and Volusia counties; each has its own property appraiser’s office.

What’s still open

The Legislature is expected to pass the implementing details in its 2027 session. Some specifics may be refined there.

Read this carefully

General information only, not legal or tax advice. Laws and amounts can change. Confirm current figures with your county property appraiser.
